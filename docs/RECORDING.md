# Recording Zara — a one-page guide

You are recording the voice of Zara, a tutor who teaches one Grade 5 child
at a time. Not a narrator, not an announcer: a patient teacher sitting
beside one child, who has all the time in the world for them.

Open **`/portal`** on the site. Enter the password once — the device
remembers it for 30 days.

---

## The room, before you start

The room matters more than the microphone.

- **Soft, not empty.** A room with a bed, curtains and a wardrobe is
  better than a large tidy one. Hang a thick blanket behind you and to one
  side if you can — you are trying to stop sound bouncing off flat walls.
- **30 cm from the mic**, roughly a spread hand. Speak slightly across it
  rather than straight into it, so your *p* and *b* sounds don't thump.
- **Same position every session.** Mark where the mic and your chair go.
  The recordings have to sound like one continuous person even though they
  were made across several days — this one habit matters more than
  anything else in this guide.
- **Off:** fan, air conditioner, fridge if you can reach it, phone
  notifications. A fan you stop hearing after ten minutes is still on the
  recording.
- Check the **level meter** at the top of the portal before you record. It
  should sit in the green while you talk. If it turns **red**, move back.
  If it warns that the room is noisy, add a blanket.

## Warm up — five minutes, every time

Cold voice is audible, and re-recording the first ten lines is a waste of
your session.

1. Hum gently up and down, twice.
2. Say a few Sinhala tongue-twisters, or count `එක, දෙක, තුන…` up to twenty.
3. Drink water at room temperature. Avoid milk and anything sugary.
4. Record the first line, listen back, and re-record it. It will be better
   the second time, and now you are warm.

## The keys

Your hands should not have to move.

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| **space** | Start recording (after a 3-2-1 countdown) · press again to stop |
| **enter** | Keep the take and move to the next line |
| **r** | Re-take — throws the last one away and counts you in again |
| **←  →** | Previous / next line |

You do not have to press stop. Recording ends on its own after **2.5
seconds** of silence, so finish the line and just breathe out.

It will not cut you off mid-line:

- it never stops in the first 3 seconds of a take,
- it never stops before you have actually started speaking,
- it listens to *your* room — it measures the quiet in the first second of
  each take, so a soft delivery in a very quiet room still counts as
  speech,
- in the last second before it stops, a **red ring** appears. If you are
  only pausing for effect, keep talking and it goes away.

If it still gets in your way — long dramatic pauses, or a very quiet
delivery — untick **auto-stop** at the top of the screen and press space
to stop each take yourself. The setting stays off for the session.

After every take it plays back to you once, automatically. Then: **keep**,
**re-take**, or **trim**.

## The other buttons

- **Previous → this** — plays the last line you kept, running straight
  into this one, so you can hear whether the rhythm carries across.
- **Reference** — plays the machine version of the same line. Useful for
  pacing when a line is long, but do not copy it; your reading is better.
- **Trim** — a waveform with a handle at each end. Drag them in to cut a
  breath at the start or a click at the end. Nothing is destroyed: the
  full recording is always kept, so you can move the handles again later.
- **☰ Lines** — every line in the lesson with its status. Tap any of them
  to jump back and re-record it. Green means kept.

## When a line reads badly

Some lines will feel wrong in your mouth. Sinhala on paper is not Sinhala
out loud, and you will notice this before anyone else does. Two ways to
say so — please use them rather than forcing a line that fights you.

**Leave a note.** The text box under the line. Write what is wrong:
*"nobody says this"*, *"too long for one breath"*, *"say X instead"*. It
reaches the writer with the line attached; you do not need to send
anything.

**Or say it your way.** Record the line in your own words. The system
checks two things automatically:

- **Every number must be said exactly as written.** This is the one thing
  that cannot bend — a misread number teaches a child something false.
- **The line must still do the same job**: ask the same question, expect
  the same answer, claim nothing new.

If it passes, *your* wording becomes the line. If it doesn't, you are told
exactly what changed — a number, or the meaning — and you can keep the
original or try again.

## How long to record

**Work in blocks of about 40 minutes, then take 10 minutes off.** Two
hours is a full session; past that your voice tires in ways you cannot
hear but the recording keeps.

- Stop and drink water at every break, and re-check the mic position.
- If you notice yourself speeding up, stop. Tiredness sounds like hurry.
- It is fine to end a session halfway through the lesson. Everything you
  have kept is saved on the server — not in the browser — so you can close
  the tab, change device, and pick up exactly where you left off. You can
  start on a laptop and finish on a phone.

If your internet drops, keep going: uploads retry on their own, and a line
is only marked green once it is genuinely saved. If a line will not turn
green, the message under the buttons says exactly why — if it mentions the
audio format or the bucket, that is ours to fix, not yours: send us the
message and move on to the next line.

## What "good" sounds like here

- **Slow.** Slower than feels natural. The child is nine and is doing
  arithmetic while listening.
- **Warm, not bright.** You are pleased to see this child.
- **Questions go up and then wait.** When you ask something, leave the
  silence in. The child answers into that gap.
- **Numbers get room.** Say them a little slower than the words around
  them, especially the long ones.
- **Praise is quiet.** "හරියටම හරි" said warmly beats anything performed.
